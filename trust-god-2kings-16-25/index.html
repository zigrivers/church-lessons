The offer

Jerusalem, 701 BC

An undefeated empire has absorbed every nearby nation. Its field commander offers food, safety, and survival if Jerusalem surrenders. Your own king says to wait.

You get one recommendation. What is it?

What evidence would you need?

What makes confidence trustworthy?

Assyria is not bluffing about its record. It has conquered the northern kingdom and many fortified cities in Judah. The pressure is persuasive because the danger is real.

Does a real danger deserve the final word?

Teacher cue

Count the show of hands without praising a choice. Ask two students with different answers what evidence could change their minds. Do not reveal the outcome yet.

The strategic picture

The board

Assyria has earned the room’s fear.

Already happened

Samaria has fallen.

The northern kingdom’s defeat is recent history, not a hypothetical warning.

Happening now

Judah’s fortified cities have fallen.

Jerusalem’s defenses are the exception, not the regional pattern.

Still unresolved

Jerusalem remains.

The city has not been delivered—but it has not surrendered either.

If Assyria’s track record is real, is fear irrational?

Faith does not require calling a strong army weak. The question is whether visible power is the only power that matters.

Am I trusting God—or only hoping no one asks me a hard question?

What can Hezekiah’s actions teach me?

  1. Pause. Do not let intimidation choose the frame or deadline.
  2. Go to the Lord. Bring Him the real message, not a cleaned-up version.
  3. Seek truth. Listen to prophetic and scriptural counsel instead of the loudest voice.
  4. Act faithfully. Do the next right thing before you know how the story ends.

Discuss the scenario, not someone’s private story. Two minutes; one question is enough.

What hearing produces

Read or reform?

Josiah does not confuse being moved with being changed.

Discover

Truth reenters the room.

The book is found where worship had continued without its corrective voice.

Humble

He lets the words accuse the present.

Josiah’s first response is not a speech. It is a heart willing to be corrected.

Inquire

Feeling becomes a search for truth.

He asks what the Lord requires instead of inventing a comfortable next step.

Gather

Private discovery becomes shared hearing.

Leaders and people hear the covenant words together.

Covenant

Agreement becomes allegiance.

Josiah commits to walk after the Lord with his whole heart and soul.

Remove

Commitment changes the environment.

Objects, practices, and leaders that sustained idolatry are removed.

Discover → humble yourself → inquire → gather → covenant → remove

Skipping the final steps turns conviction into a feeling that expires.
